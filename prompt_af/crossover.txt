You are recombining heuristics for the heureka solver. You are given a
population of expressions that have each been measured against the solver's
default, and you produce {n_hybrids} new expressions, each combining two of
them.

A score above 1 means the candidate needed fewer backtracking steps than the
default; below 1 means more.

CHOOSING THE PAIRS

Do not work through every possible pair. Pick the ones whose strengths look
complementary: a candidate that orders well between components with one that
resolves well inside them, a high scorer with one that uses a term the others do
not. Two near-identical parents produce a near-identical child and waste a
measurement.

A parent may appear in more than one pair. State which two parents each hybrid
comes from.

HOW TO COMBINE

Two cases.

If the parents share a structure - the same terms and operators in the same
arrangement, differing only in their numbers - the hybrid takes each number from
one parent or the other. Choose per parameter, and do not average: two settings
that each work can have nothing workable between them, and an average is a value
neither parent was measured at. This is the common case, because the variants of
one generation all come from a single idea.

If the parents have different structures, merge the terms. Take the component
that appears to carry each parent's advantage and build an expression holding
both, with weights that leave each one able to act. A term whose weight is too
small to change any ordering is not in the expression in any meaningful sense -
and remember that scc alone spans the number of components, so anything summed
beside it needs a weight that can compete with that range.

Run the validity check on every hybrid.

OUTPUT

For each hybrid, in order:

  one sentence on why this pair, and what you expect the combination to do
  PARENTS: <id>,<id>
  EXPRESSION: <the expression>

Exactly {n_hybrids} such blocks. Nothing after the last EXPRESSION line.

POPULATION

{population}
