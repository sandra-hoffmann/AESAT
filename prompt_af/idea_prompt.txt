You are an expert on search heuristics for abstract argumentation solvers.

The solver enumerates complete/preferred extensions with a backtracking
search. Before the search, a heuristic assigns a numeric score to every
argument, and the arguments are then tried in order of DESCENDING score:
the argument with the highest score is branched on first. A good heuristic
therefore gives high scores to arguments that are likely to be in an
extension, so that the solver needs fewer backtracking steps.

Background: heuristic components known from the literature on this solver

Each component maps an argument a to a number. E is the partial extension built
so far during the search. {{a}}- are the arguments attacking a, {{a}}+ the
arguments attacked by a, and E+ the arguments attacked by E.

Degree ratio
   h_ratio(E, a) = (|{{a}}+| + eps) / (|{{a}}-| + eps)
   Outgoing attacks divided by incoming attacks, with a small constant eps to
   keep the quotient defined. Independent of E.

Path-based components
   Let d+_i(a) be the number of paths of length i starting at a, and d-_i(a) the
   number of paths of length i ending at a.
   h_path+(a) = sum over i = 1..k of alpha_i * d+_i(a)
   h_path-(a) = sum over i = 1..k of beta_i  * d-_i(a)
   The depth k and the weights alpha, beta are parameters. Outgoing paths
   measure how far a's influence reaches, incoming paths how exposed it is.
   Common combinations:
     h_path_sum  = h_path- + h_path+
     h_path_prod = (-1) * (h_path- + eps) * (h_path+ + eps)

Graph centralities
   Betweenness centrality, eigenvector centrality and the matrix exponential of
   the attack relation, each used as a component on its own.

SCC ordering
   Arguments are ordered by the position of their strongly connected component
   in a topological ordering of the condensation, following the idea of
   SCC-recursiveness. Arguments in earlier components are decided first.

Components can be combined arithmetically into compound heuristics; that is how
the strong heuristics in this family are built.
