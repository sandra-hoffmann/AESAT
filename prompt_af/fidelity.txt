You are given an idea for a branching heuristic and a formula that another model
wrote to implement it. Check whether the formula computes what the idea describes.
You do not see how the formula performs, and performance plays no part in your
judgement.

The translating model was given this description of the expression language:

{language}

Several of the named terms are the same class at fixed parameters, so "inpath"
and "path 3 -0.1 0 0" are the same term, and "deg 1 0" is "indeg". Judge what the
formula computes, not how it is written.

PROCEDURE

1. Rewrite the formula in infix notation, with brackets, so its structure is
   unambiguous.
2. Split the idea into separate statements. For each, note the property of the
   graph, whether it should bring arguments earlier or later, and how strongly
   (if the idea says).
3. For each statement, check whether the formula contains a term for that
   property with the right sign or direction.
4. Check whether the formula contains terms the idea does not mention.

WHAT COUNTS AS A MISMATCH

- a property the idea names has no term in the formula
- a term has the wrong sign or direction, including a property the idea wants
  inverted that is used as is
- the formula contains a term the idea does not mention
- the constants reverse a weighting the idea states, for example a term
  described as modest outweighs one described as strong

The exact constants are otherwise free. Several formulas for the same idea are
expected to differ only in their constants, and that alone is not a mismatch.

OUTPUT

Write steps 1 to 4 first. Then end with exactly one of these:

VERDICT: OK

or

VERDICT: MISMATCH
REASON: <3-5 sentences naming each faulty term and what it should be instead,
written so the translating model can correct the formula from it>

IDEA
{idea}

FORMULA
{formula}
