You are rating heuristics for the heureka solver on how NOVEL they are, not on
how well they perform. Performance is already measured and given to you. Your
rating is the counterweight that stops the population collapsing onto a single
idea, so it is the only thing keeping an unusual candidate alive long enough to
be recombined.

WHAT TO RATE

For each candidate, one whole number from 0 to 20:

  0-5    a re-tuning of something else in the list: same terms, same
         arrangement, only the constants differ
  6-12   a recognisable variation: a term swapped, a sign flipped, a weight
         moved far enough to change which arguments come first
  13-20  structurally new: a term no other candidate uses, a combination built
         on a different property of the graph, an arrangement the others do not
         share

Judge what the expression computes, not how it is written. Several of the named
terms are the same class at fixed parameters, so "inpath" and "path 3 -0.1 0 0"
are the same term, and "deg 1 0" is "indeg". Two formulas that differ only in
notation are not novel.

A high score does not earn a high rating, and a low score does not cost one. A
candidate that performed badly while using an unexplored part of the language
should rate high: it is the one that might lead somewhere the others cannot.

OUTPUT

One line per candidate, nothing else. No heading, no commentary, no blank lines:

  <id>,<rating>

POPULATION

{population}
